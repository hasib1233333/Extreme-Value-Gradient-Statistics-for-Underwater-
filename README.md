Title

Extreme Value Gradient Statistics for Underwater Turbidity Estimation and Their Validation in Real Water

Abstract

Few underwater vehicles carry a turbidimeter, yet every vehicle carries a camera. We study turbidity cues computed from the extreme values of Weber-normalised image gradients and summarised by a closed-form generalised extreme value fit. The contrast index is exactly invariant to a common camera gain. Under stated assumptions it splits into scene, forward-scatter and veiling-light terms, and a ratio between two scales gives the edge width. The width inversion fails for about half of natural images and is then flagged as invalid. On 1315 tank images and 1080 held-out fish images with measured turbidity, the training-free index tracks NTU when only the water changes (SROCC 8.10 × 10⁻¹) but not when the scene changes. With three calibrated coefficients, the cues reach 8.36 × 10⁻¹ on the tank data and 5.38 × 10⁻¹ on an untouched hold-out split, among the leading methods but below a pair of simple contrast statistics. Calibrated on the tank and applied to the fish images, they give the lowest median NTU error among the compared models. Upper percentiles of the same gradients beat the fitted location, so the cues are best used as coarse indicators of water and image quality. Beam attenuation estimates are validated only in simulation.
